Computer-use agent benchmarks

Computer-use benchmarks are task suites and environments that score agents operating web browsers, desktop applications or mobile devices. Each pairs a fixed set of tasks with an evaluator, such as execution-based state checks, answer matching or target localization. The catalog lists 300 reviewed benchmarks, each linked to its primary evidence.

Questions

Which benchmarks are mentioned most in the catalog?

OSWorld (60 records), WebArena (30 records) and AndroidWorld (21 records) are mentioned in the reviewed sources of the most other catalog records.

Which platforms do these benchmarks cover?

Of 300 reviewed benchmarks, 207 are tagged web, 94 desktop, 43 mobile and 28 cross-platform; 12 have no platform recorded. An entry tagged with several platforms counts under each.

Which benchmarks have an official leaderboard?

25 of 300 benchmarks link an official leaderboard from their reviewed source, including OSWorld, WebArena, AndroidWorld and ScreenSpot Pro.

How many benchmarks are interactive and how many offline?

Of 94 benchmarks with a recorded protocol, 56 are interactive, 34 are offline and 4 are mixed. The benchmark comparison page lists each protocol's observations, actions, evaluator and counts.

Which licenses do benchmarks declare?

The most common declared licenses are MIT (118), Apache-2.0 (70) and CC-BY-4.0 (7). 96 of 300 have no declared license in their record.
